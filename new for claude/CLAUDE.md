# Project Overview: IEEE Micromouse Simulator & Telemetry Dashboard

Act as an expert Python/C++ developer and Machine Learning engineer specializing in Reinforcement Learning and embedded robotics. 

I am building a Micromouse simulator with a clean, multi-file Object-Oriented Programming (OOP) architecture. The mouse drives continuously (position in cm, heading angle, limited acceleration / speed, in-place turns), maps the maze only from noisy sensor distances, and navigates with flood fill (least-turn Dijkstra / BFS for path analysis) across a 3-round search system.

## Current Architecture
The project is split into 8 files (the 7 below plus `body.py`, the simulated physical robot - see "Drift & centering"):
1. `config.py`: All constants: 10x10 grid (kept on purpose), real-world sizes (MMRC26: cells 18 cm INSIDE the walls, 1.2 cm walls, so `CELL_SIZE_CM` = 19.2 cm pitch; 8x10 cm mouse), the `SENSORS` layout (IR range 2.5 cm, TOF 80 cm), sensor rate / noise / dropout, mapping evidence thresholds, motion limits, physics rate, sim speeds, Pygame colors. `CELL_SIZE` is the only drawing-scale knob.
2. `cell.py`: Defines the `Cell` class (walls, `walls_known`, visited status).
3. `maze.py`: Defines the `Maze` class: generates mazes that follow the MMRC26 rules (3-walled start cell open to the North, island 2x2 goal with one entrance, a wall on every post, loops); `rule_violations()` checks them all, including left/right wall-follower tests. Also BFS shortest path and least-turn Dijkstra. Wall keys ('H', x, j) / ('V', i, y) are shared with mapper.py.
4. `sensors.py`: `SensorArray.scan(px, py, theta)` casts each beam (numpy slab test) against real wall/post boxes and returns `Reading`s carrying ONLY a noisy distance (+ where the sensor is/points). No ground truth (which wall was hit) leaks out.
5. `mapper.py`: `Mapper` turns readings into per-edge evidence scores: beam end on a wall face = wall vote, beam passing a slot = open vote, driving through = certain open. Ambiguous hits (posts, corners) and one-sample dropouts are ignored. An edge is known at |score| >= `WALL_EVIDENCE_THRESHOLD`.
6. `micromouse.py`: The `Micromouse`: `update(physical_maze, dt)` per physics tick. Senses at `SENSOR_HZ`; always brakes towards a target cell centre and, near the braking point, decides: straight on through a confirmed-open edge (target extends, no stop) or stop at the centre and turn in place. Only ever drives through confirmed-open edges. Rounds restart by hand-placing at the start.
7. `main.py`: The `DashboardApp` Pygame UI: "The Maze" (ground truth), "Running Maze" (the mouse's map, sensor hit point cloud, live beams), right sidebar with on/off overlay buttons + run stats + sensor readings, blue Regenerate / Start buttons at the bottom. Up/Down arrows change sim speed (x0.25 - x8).

Validation used so far: headless runs over 200 random mazes -> 0 collisions, 0 wrongly-mapped edges, 0 failed rounds.

## Drive train & strategy
Two wheels on the body's centre line, 34 mm wheels, motors 381 rpm at the wheel -> top speed ≈ 67.8 cm/s. Encoders on each motor, no IMU. Accel 200 cm/s² and wheel track 7 cm are assumptions (in `config.py`). Speed: wheels run at full 381 rpm everywhere: 67.8 cm/s straights (search runs too, through cells already mapped; `EXPLORE_SPEED_CM_S` 40 cm/s only when heading into a cell whose walls aren't all confirmed) and corners at `CORNER_SPEED_CM_S` ≈ 49.7 cm/s (outer wheel at 381 rpm, 0.26 g). Steering gains retuned for that speed (CENTER_KP 1.2, CENTER_KH 14). Rounds: SEARCH (flood fill) until the map proves the best route, then FAST (Dijkstra quickest route over confirmed-open edges, cost = cell time + corner time, U-turn = pivot time; re-plans if the map changes). The last round is always FAST. Keep the bot logic small and C++/ESP32-portable (user request). Strategy notes from the rulebook (score = runs / best time): see the chat plan - smooth turns first, then official timing / flying start / match mode.

Turns: corners are SMOOTH quarter circles (motion "ARC") of radius half a cell (`TURN_RADIUS_CM` 9.6) centred on the corner post, from the entry-edge middle to the exit-edge middle, without stopping, at `CORNER_SPEED_CM_S` (outer wheel at 381 rpm). The move is decided at `_decision_distance()` before the target cell; an arc needs the exit edge confirmed open. Arcs are tracked on the encoder-dead-reckoned pose (`_arc_omega`: radius + tangent feedback) and start/end `MOTOR_LAG_COMP_S` early. Pivot turns on the spot remain only for U-turns / dead ends / unconfirmed exits. Fast run went from ≈8.2 s (pivots) to ≈3.6 s (40/45 cm/s corners) to ≈3.5 s at full 381 rpm; round 1 search ≈7.2 s.

## Drift & centering (reality gap)
`body.py` is the TRUE robot (simulation only): motor lag (`MOTOR_TAU_S` 30 ms), per-robot wheel-diameter mismatch, wheel slip, hand-placement error, TOF readings that describe the pose `SENSOR_LATENCY_S` (20 ms) ago. `body.step()` returns what the ENCODERS measured; the firmware (`micromouse.py`) builds its believed pose only from those (`_odometry`) and places each reading at its believed pose 20 ms ago (`pose_hist`). Localisation (`_observe_walls`): every TOF reading up to `MAP_TRUST_CM` (25 cm) is compared with a ray-cast of the mouse's CONFIRMED walls from the believed pose (`SensorArray(memory, known_only=True)`); a wall along the lane corrects the observer (offset, heading, learned wheel curve), a wall across the lane corrects the along-lane position; posts / unexplained readings are skipped. The 2.5 cm IR is used directly. Front-wall re-centring (averaged front TOF) at every stop / after every pivot. Any body point inside a wall/post = crash (round lost).
Measured (headless, 200 rule-compliant mazes / 600 runs, no IMU, full 381 rpm): ≈6.5% of runs crash, 0.66 cm RMS off-centre. Higher speed alone raised crashes (fewer readings per cm, lag/latency cost more distance); stronger steering gains brought it back. Stronger OBSERVER gains go unstable - don't. Noise-source test (200 mazes): removing the wheel-size mismatch halves crashes (39 -> ~23); slip, placement and TOF noise barely matter. Most crashes are the 2nd corner of a back-to-back pair after drifting through wall-less stretches. Tried and REJECTED (same seeds, all worse): slower chained corners (35-45 cm/s), arc curvature from measured speed, earlier decisions, learned curve inside arcs. Fix is calibration / hardware (IMU, longer side IR). SECOND crash cause (fixed): corners committed through side walls the map wrongly had as OPEN (19 of ~3000 corners; straights: 0). Wrong open votes came from real hits placed at a slightly wrong pose, mostly while rotating. Fixes: no mapping during ARC/TURN; open needs -OPEN_EVIDENCE_THRESHOLD (8) vs wall +3; 'drove through' is a -DRIVEN_VOTE (8) vote, not an override. Result: 4% of runs crash (was 6.5%), round-1 search ~9.9 s (was 7.2, more look-stops), fast run unchanged ~3.5 s. Diagonal runs through staircases were requested and are the next feature (paused to investigate crashes): diagonal clearance to posts is only ~1.9 cm per side. Most remaining crashes are corners taken in open areas (no walls to localise on for >1 s). Tried and rejected (no gain): an IR steer-away reflex during arcs, longer trust ranges, stronger along gain. The real fixes are hardware: an IMU and/or a longer-range side IR.
Dashboard: The Maze shows the REAL body + its path (cyan, "Real Path"), red on crash; Running Maze shows the BELIEVED pose (incl. observer estimates).

## Sensor layout (from the user's hardware sketch)
2 side IR distance sensors pointing straight left/right; 3 TOF at the rear: one centre pointing forward, two at the rear corners angled 45° across the body (beams cross at the centre). Defined in `config.SENSORS`. With 2.5 cm range the side IR never reaches a wall while the mouse is centred (wall face is ~4.9 cm away); the diagonal TOFs are what map the side walls.
